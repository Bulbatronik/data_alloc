# Annotated references: SFT vs. RL data allocation

Compiled 2026-09-27. Every paper below was checked against its arXiv, ACL Anthology, or conference page. Numbers come from the papers' abstracts or HTML full text. Anything I could not verify is marked **[unverified]**.

**Project recap.** We split a fixed pool of N GSM8K training problems into an SFT bucket (gold traces, LoRA, 1 epoch) and a disjoint GRPO bucket (binary reward, 8 samples per prompt). On Qwen2.5-0.5B base (N=1000, 3 seeds, untrained model at 20.9%):

- Full GRPO: 42.6%
- 250 SFT / 750 GRPO: 43.0% with random allocation, 44.1% with adaptive allocation
- 500/500: about 40.7%
- 750/250: about 37.5%
- Full SFT: 34.4%

The SFT gain saturates at about +13 points. The GRPO gain keeps growing with the number of prompts. Adaptive vs. random routing: +0.4 ± 0.6 points. The router's p is dominated by format failures (334/1000 problems have p=0).

## (A) Directly on SFT/RL budget allocation

### A1. Scaling Near-Optimal SFT–RL Annotation Budget Allocation from Small to Large LLMs
Wang (Jingtan) et al., 2026. EMNLP 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01573

- **Core idea.** Given a fixed annotation budget, they split it between SFT and RL using the SFT-fraction grid r ∈ {0, .25, .5, .75, 1}, with LoRA. Instead of looking for one optimal ratio, they describe the "near-optimal region": all ratios that score within ε of the best one. They then test whether that region transfers from small proxy models to large ones.
- **Key findings.**
  - Setup: Llama 3, Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 models up to 8B (14B in the appendix). Tasks: GSM8K, instruction following, summarization, helpfulness. RL methods: DPO and GRPO. Budgets up to about 15k samples.
  - Width of the region: at ε=10%, most tasks have near-optimal ratios covering 55–75% of the allocation space.
  - Scale: the region widens as the model gets larger.
  - Transfer: a 3-step small-to-large procedure succeeds 94.3% of the time at ε=5% and 97.1% at ε=10%.
  - Cost: if SFT labels cost 2–10× more than RL labels, the scaling trends still hold.
  - Pure RL: "Allocating too few samples to SFT leaves a weak initial policy that RL cannot effectively improve." They drop r=0 from the cost analysis because it "consistently underperforms."
  - For GSM8K, the RL stage reportedly uses Tülu-3 grade-school-math prompts rather than the GSM8K train set.
  - **[unverified]** I could not find per-ratio GSM8K accuracies in the main text.
- **Relevance.**
  - *Agrees:* our region is also wide (0/1000 and 250/750 are within about 1.5 points).
  - *Contradicts:* in our runs pure RL is near-optimal, not clearly worse. The likely reason is that Qwen2.5-0.5B base already partly follows the answer format (see D1, D2).
  - *Suggests:*
    - Report our results as ε-regions rather than an argmax.
    - Test whether the region found on 0.5B transfers to the Instruct and Llama models (their "proxy → target" test).

### A2. Balancing the Budget: Understanding Trade-offs Between Supervised and Preference-Based Finetuning
Raghavendra et al., 2025. ACL 2025 (long; SAC award). https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1248/ (arXiv 2502.11284)

- **Core idea.** How to split a fixed budget between SFT and preference finetuning (mainly DPO, with KTO also tested). Setup:
  - Models: Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, plus ablations at 1B–3B.
  - Training: LoRA.
  - Tasks: four, including GSM8K.
  - Budgets: 100 to 20k examples.
- **Key findings.**
  - Below about 1k examples, SFT dominates.
  - At larger budgets, the best mix shifts toward preference data (e.g., about 3:1 PFT:SFT).
  - Running PFT straight on the base model gives a "cold start": little or no gain for small models on math. Spending **<10% of the budget on SFT first gives 15–20% improvements on GSM8K**.
  - Their explanation: the step-by-step response style is far from the base model's behaviour, and a little SFT aligns the format.
  - Their GSM8K preference pairs were generated by GPT-4o and scored with a rubric, so they are off-policy. That differs from our on-policy GRPO.
- **Relevance.**
  - *Agrees:* a small SFT slice plus a mostly-RL budget is best. That matches our 250/750 peak.
  - *Explains:* SFT's value is mostly about format. That fits our saturation at about +13 points.
  - *Suggests:* the denser grid near 0–250 is well motivated. Add points at 10, 25, 50 and 100 SFT examples, since their gains appear already at <10%.

### A3. Reinforcement Learning for Out-of-Distribution Reasoning in LLMs: An Empirical Study on Diagnosis-Related Group Coding
Wang (Hanyin) et al., 2025. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21908

- **Core idea.** DRG-Sapphire: Qwen2.5-7B trained with SFT then GRPO for medical DRG coding. It includes an ablation of how to split a fixed dataset between SFT and GRPO.
- **Key findings.**
  - On DRG-Small (46.8k cases), GRPO adds about +10 points Pass@1 over SFT at every split tested.
  - On the full 236k set, they tried 50/50, 75/25, 90/10 and 95/5 SFT/RL. **90% SFT / 10% RL was best (54.8%).**
  - Both SFT and GRPO performance scale roughly linearly in log(#SFT examples).
  - GRPO alone on vanilla Qwen2.5 failed to produce correct codes.
  - RL raised Pass@1 but lowered Pass@8, i.e. it sharpens rather than expands.
- **Relevance.**
  - *Opposite optimum to ours:* SFT-heavy. This is a knowledge-heavy task where the base model lacks domain knowledge. On GSM8K the base model already "knows" enough, so RL-heavy wins.
  - *Suggests:* the optimum depends on how much of the task the base model can already solve. That is an argument for running the Instruct and Llama models.
  - *Suggests:* also record pass@8 on the test set to see whether our GRPO gains are just sharpening.

## (B) Routing / mixing SFT and RL by difficulty

### B1. Learning What to Learn: Stage-Specific Data Sets for SFT-then-RL in Small Language Model Reasoning
He (Chongyang) et al., 2026. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04466

- **Core idea.** Their thesis: "SFT is better suited for acquiring not-yet-mastered reasoning skills, while RL is better suited for consolidating skills the model can already partially access." They split GSM8K by the model's own pass rate over 64 samples: Simple ≥0.75, Medium 0.25–0.75, Hard <0.25.
  - SFT gets Medium problems plus "Bridge"-restructured Hard teacher traces. The teacher is Qwen-Plus. The Bridge step keeps, compresses, expands, drops or localizes individual steps.
  - RL (GRPO) gets the prompts that are not all-zero reward.
  - Prompts that stay all-zero after RL are recycled into Critique Fine-Tuning data for the next SFT round. The loop runs for 2 iterations.
- **Key findings.**
  - Models: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Llama3.2-1B-Instruct. Training on GSM8K only; evaluation on GSM8K-Platinum, MAWPS, SVAMP, MATH500 and LogiQA.
  - Qwen-0.5B-Instruct on GSM8K-Platinum: base 45.7%, GRPO-only 51.0%, theirs 54.5%.
  - 5-benchmark average: 49.7% (Qwen) and 48.5% (Llama).
  - Motivating experiment: SFT on a Medium→Hard curriculum beats SFT on any single difficulty group or on a mixed set, measured by pass@8 after SFT. SFT on Hard alone is weakest.
- **Relevance.**
  - This is the closest match to our next step: same two models, GSM8K.
  - *Contradicts our router:* it sends the lowest-p problems (mostly p=0) to SFT. They find raw Hard traces are the *least* useful SFT data, while Medium plus bridged Hard helps.
  - *Suggests:*
    - Probe with more samples: 64 rather than 8, so p is less noisy.
    - Try a rule that sends mid-p problems to SFT.
    - Treat "better SFT traces" (teacher or bridged) as part of routing, not a separate project.
    - Their GRPO-only number gives a reference point for our Instruct runs. Note that it is measured on GSM8K-Platinum, not the standard test set.

### B2. Learning What Reinforcement Learning Can't: Interleaved Online Fine-Tuning for Hardest Questions (ReLIFT)
Ma (Lu) et al., 2025. ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07527

- **Core idea.** During RL, questions where all N rollouts fail (acc=0) are collected. Correct demonstrations for them (from DeepSeek-R1 or humans) are used in interleaved SFT steps. SFT happens more often early in training and RL dominates later.
- **Key findings.**
  - RL keeps performance on questions within the model's reach; SFT is what makes progress on questions beyond it.
  - +5.2 points average over five competition benchmarks.
  - Qwen2.5-Math-7B: 52.6% vs. pure RL 46.9%, pure SFT 49.0%, LUFFY 50.9%. It uses only about 13% of the demonstration data.
  - Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: 36.5% vs. RL 34.2%.
- **Relevance.**
  - *Supports* sending p=0 problems to SFT, but decided **online** on the *current* policy, not once on the untrained model.
  - Our router probes before any training, so after SFT fixes the format many "p=0" problems are no longer hard. That may be why the adaptive effect is null.
  - *Suggests:* re-probe after the SFT stage, or run an interleaved variant where each GRPO batch's all-zero prompts get an SFT step on their gold trace.

### B3. Learning to Reason under Off-Policy Guidance (LUFFY)
Yan (Jianhao) et al., 2025. NeurIPS 2025 (the arXiv v5 comment still says "work in progress"; acceptance confirmed on neurips.cc). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14945

- **Core idea.**
  - Mixed-policy GRPO: DeepSeek-R1 traces (OpenR1-Math, 45k prompts) are added to each prompt's group of on-policy rollouts.
  - "Policy shaping" reweights gradients with regularized importance sampling f(x)=x/(x+γ). This boosts low-probability tokens and avoids rigid imitation.
- **Key findings.**
  - +6.4 points average over six math benchmarks and +6.2 points out-of-distribution vs. prior RLVR methods.
  - Beats SFT→RL while using about 59% of the GPU hours.
  - Trains weak models such as Llama-3.1-8B on hard data, where on-policy RL's reward collapses to zero.
- **Relevance.**
  - A third option beside "SFT bucket vs. GRPO bucket": give a problem to GRPO *with* its gold trace injected as an off-policy rollout. For p=0 prompts, which currently give zero GRPO gradient, this recovers signal without a separate SFT stage.
  - *Suggests:* a single-bucket baseline at the same N. All 1000 problems go to GRPO, and gold traces are injected only when all 8 rollouts fail. That tests whether the split matters at all.

### B4. (extra) Towards a Unified View of Large Language Model Post-Training (HPT)
Lv (Xingtai) et al., 2025. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04419

- **Core idea.** Hybrid Post-Training. For each question, sample n=8 rollouts. If the mean score P ≤ γ, do SFT on the demonstration; otherwise do RL. γ=0 for Qwen and 2/8 for LLaMA.
- **Key findings.**
  - Qwen2.5-Math-7B, AIME24: 33.0% vs. SFT→GRPO 25.7% and LUFFY 26.1%.
  - Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B average: 41.9% vs. LUFFY 34.7%.
  - LLaMA-3.1-8B average: 18.2% vs. LUFFY 13.2%.
- **Relevance.**
  - Almost the same routing statistic as ours (8-sample pass rate), but applied **online** throughout training. The larger γ for the weaker model family is a useful detail.
  - *Suggests:* an "online-gated" variant as the natural next step if static routing stays null. Our static router is essentially HPT's gate frozen at step 0.

## (C) What SFT and RL each contribute

### C1. SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes: A Comparative Study of Foundation Model Post-training
Chu et al., 2025. ICML 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161

- **Core idea.** Compares SFT and RL (outcome reward) on Llama-3.2-Vision-11B. Tasks: GeneralPoints (an arithmetic card game) and V-IRL navigation. Each has out-of-distribution rule and visual variants.
- **Key findings.**
  - RL generalizes and SFT memorizes. On GeneralPoints rule-OOD: RL +3.5 points (11.5→15.0), SFT −8.1 points (11.5→3.4). On V-IRL rule-OOD: RL +11.0, SFT −79.5.
  - But "SFT remains essential for effective RL training; SFT stabilizes the model's output format." End-to-end RL from the base model failed completely in their setup.
- **Relevance.** Consistent with ours: SFT's contribution saturates (format), and further gains come from RL. Their RL-without-SFT failure did not happen for us on Qwen2.5-0.5B base. It may happen on Llama-3.2-1B.
  - *Suggests:* measure format-compliance rate separately from answer accuracy after each stage, to test the "SFT = format" reading directly.

### C2. Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?
Yue (Yang) et al., 2025. NeurIPS 2025 (oral). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837

- **Core idea.** Compares pass@k of base and RLVR-trained models for large k. The models are Qwen2.5 7B/14B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B and others; the RL algorithms are PPO, GRPO, Reinforce++, RLOO, ReMax and DAPO.
- **Key findings.**
  - RL wins at small k, but base models catch up and overtake as k grows to the tens or hundreds. For example, 32B on Minerva: base about 9 points higher at k=128.
  - On easier sets like GSM8K the crossover comes at small k.
  - RLVR's reasoning is "bounded by the base model." Distillation from a stronger teacher does expand coverage.
- **Relevance.** This explains why GRPO keeps improving with more prompts but only up to the base model's coverage. It also explains why higher-quality SFT traces are the lever for going beyond that.
  - *Suggests:* log pass@k (k up to 64 or 256) for the untrained, SFT-only and GRPO-only models on the test set, to see which stage expands coverage.

### C3. (extra) Towards Revealing the Effectiveness of Small-Scale Fine-Tuning in R1-style Reinforcement Learning
Chen (Yutong) et al., 2025. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17988

- **Core idea.** Analyses why a small SFT stage strongly affects later RL. Proposes "Re-distillation": SFT on samples drawn from an RL-trained policy.
- **Key findings.** Re-distilled models match RL performance with far fewer samples and less compute. A Qwen2.5-1.5B trained on 1k re-distilled samples beats DeepSeek-V3-0324 on K&K (Knights and Knaves logic puzzles). Results hold across Qwen and Llama models.
- **Relevance.** Directly relevant to the planned "self-generated SFT traces": the SFT bucket could use traces from our own GRPO-trained model instead of GSM8K gold traces.
  - *Suggests:* a "GSM8K gold vs. self-generated (rejection-sampled or re-distilled) vs. teacher" trace comparison at fixed 250 SFT.

### C4. DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning
DeepSeek-AI (Guo et al.), 2025. arXiv 2501.12948; later published in Nature (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948

- **Core idea.** R1-Zero (pure RL from the base model) reasons well but has poor readability and mixes languages. R1 adds a "cold-start" SFT on a few thousand long-CoT examples before RL, followed by further SFT and RL stages.
- **Relevance.** This is the canonical "small SFT, then big RL" recipe, and our 250/750 optimum is a miniature of it. Note that the cold-start data was selected for readability and format, which again points to format as SFT's main job.

## (D) Caveats about models and rewards

### D1. Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR
Shao (Rulin) et al., 2025. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947

- **Core idea.** GRPO on DeepScaleR data with deliberately uninformative rewards.
- **Key findings.**
  - Qwen2.5-Math-7B MATH-500 gains by reward type: ground truth +29.1, majority vote +26.4, incorrect +24.1, **random +21.4**, **format-only +18.7**.
  - Mechanism: GRPO's clipping bias amplifies behaviours the model already has (Qwen's "code reasoning" rises from 65% to over 90%).
  - Spurious rewards rarely help Llama3 or OLMo2. The authors urge testing on non-Qwen models.
- **Relevance.** A large part of our +21.7-point full-GRPO gain on Qwen2.5-0.5B may be format and prior amplification rather than learned reasoning. That fits p being dominated by format failures.
  - *Suggests:*
    - Add a format-only-reward and/or random-reward GRPO control at N=1000.
    - Treat Llama-3.2-1B as the more informative model family for the allocation question.

### D2. SimpleRL-Zoo: Investigating and Taming Zero Reinforcement Learning for Open Base Models in the Wild
Zeng (Weihao) et al., 2025. COLM 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18892

- **Core idea.** Zero-RL (GRPO from the base model, rule reward) on 10 base models, from Qwen2.5-0.5B to 32B, plus Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral. Data is split into three difficulty tiers of about 8k problems each; Easy = GSM8K + MATH level 1.
- **Key findings.**
  - **Qwen2.5-0.5B GSM8K: 36.7% → 49.5%.**
  - Strict format rewards hurt exploration and can collapse weaker models (Llama-3.1-8B).
  - Training difficulty must match the model's ability.
  - Short-CoT SFT before RL (100–500 steps) *reduced* later reasoning behaviours (verification, enumeration).
- **Relevance.**
  - Their 0.5B baseline (36.7%) is much higher than our untrained 20.9%. The eval prompt or answer extraction probably differs. **[unverified]** which exactly.
  - This supports the idea that our router's p=0 mass is a format artifact of our prompt or parser, not true difficulty.
  - Their SFT-hurts-RL finding is a caution for SFT-heavy splits (our 750/250 and 1000/0 are worst).
  - *Suggests:*
    - Check our prompt and extraction against theirs.
    - Consider a lenient answer extractor for the router's probe.

## Where this leaves us

- **Wide optimum, small SFT slice.** A1, A2 and C4 all support a wide near-optimal region with a small SFT fraction. Our 250/750 ≈ 0/1000 result is consistent with that. The open question is where the optimum sits in 0–250, so the planned dense grid (0, 10, 25, 50, 100, 150, 250) is well justified. Report results as ε-regions, not a single argmax.
- **Expect base-model dependence.** Pure RL "consistently underperforms" in A1, A2 and C1. For us it is near-optimal on Qwen2.5-0.5B. D1 and D2 suggest this is a Qwen-specific prior plus format effect. Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct is the key test, and a cold-start benefit from ~25–50 SFT examples is likely there.
- **Separate format from reasoning.** Log format-compliance and accuracy separately after each stage. Add a format-only-reward GRPO control (D1). If the SFT gain is mostly format, the SFT bucket's value is capped by construction.
- **Change how the router probes.**
  - Static routing from the untrained model is the weak point: p is a format artifact, and 8 samples are noisy.
  - Options: (a) probe after a tiny format-SFT or with a lenient parser; (b) use more samples (B1 uses 64); (c) route online during GRPO as ReLIFT/HPT do (all-zero prompts → SFT step on the gold trace).
- **Revisit which problems go to SFT.** B1 finds medium-difficulty problems (plus bridged hard ones) make better SFT data than the hardest ones. B2 and B4 favor p=0 but decide on the *current* policy. A quick ablation comparing "SFT = lowest-p" with "SFT = mid-p" at 250/750 would separate these.
- **Better traces are probably the bigger lever.** B1, B2, B3, C2 and C3 all get their gains from better or off-policy traces (teacher, bridged, or re-distilled), not from routing alone. Gold GSM8K traces are short and stylistically off-policy. Compare gold vs. self-generated (rejection-sampled) vs. teacher traces at a fixed SFT count.
- **Consider a single-bucket baseline.** A LUFFY- or HPT-style run where all N prompts go to GRPO and gold traces are used only when rollouts all fail. This tests whether a hard split of the pool is necessary at all.
- **Statistical power.** Our adaptive-vs-random difference (+0.4 ± 0.6) is below what 3 seeds can resolve. Use more seeds, paired seeds or data orders across conditions, and a paired test on the 1319 per-item outcomes before claiming a routing effect.
